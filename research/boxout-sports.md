# Competitor Research: Box Out Sports

**Website:** [boxoutsports.com](https://boxoutsports.com/) | **HQ:** South Bend / Mishawaka, Indiana | **Founded:** ~2013–2015 (sources disagree, see §4)

Box Out Sports is a real, established, self-serve template-based sports graphics platform — the clearest direct analog to the "self-serve tool" side of the competitive hypothesis. It is not a startup with thin traction; it has multi-year governing-body partnerships (NAIA, NJCAA) and a decade-plus of operating history. Confidence in the core product/pricing facts is high (sourced directly from the company's own site). Confidence in social/financial/employee numbers is lower — flagged inline.

---

## 1. What the product/service does

Box Out is a cloud-based (browser-only, no native app) template platform: users pick from a library of pre-built, fill-in-the-blank sports graphic templates, swap in their team's colors/logo/photos/stats, and export or directly post the result. It's positioned as a replacement for hiring a designer or wrestling with Photoshop/After Effects/Canva. ([boxoutsports.com](https://boxoutsports.com/), [boxoutsports.com/switch](https://boxoutsports.com/switch))

Core workflow/features:
- **Template library**: 430–2,390+ templates depending on plan tier, covering score updates, player spotlights, schedules, rankings/standings, quotes, brackets, recruiting/signing-day "credential" graphics, and QR codes, across ~17 sports. ([boxoutsports.com/pricing](https://boxoutsports.com/pricing), [boxoutsports.com/graphics](https://boxoutsports.com/graphics))
- **Multi-format output**: one edit auto-generates matching versions sized for different social platforms simultaneously ("Create Once, Get Three Formats Automatically"). ([boxoutsports.com/](https://boxoutsports.com/))
- **Logo/roster database**: a large database of team logos (13,000+ team logos claimed) plus roster import from images, PDFs, spreadsheets, or URLs, and calendar sync to auto-generate schedule/score graphics. ([boxoutsports.com/ai-info](https://boxoutsports.com/ai-info), [boxoutsports.com/features](https://boxoutsports.com/features))
- **Motion/video templates**: 30–130+ video templates depending on tier, plus a built-in video editor. ([boxoutsports.com/pricing](https://boxoutsports.com/pricing))
- **Photo editing suite** including AI background removal (limited on lower tiers, full access on Premium). ([boxoutsports.com/pricing](https://boxoutsports.com/pricing))
- **Live stats integration** — Premium tier only. ([boxoutsports.com/pricing](https://boxoutsports.com/pricing))
- **Publishing**: direct posting/scheduling to social accounts, multi-account posting, print-ready PDF export for signage, sponsor-placement automation for program revenue, and approval workflows/user permissions for staff. ([boxoutsports.com/features](https://boxoutsports.com/features))
- **Branded web pages**: custom program microsites, schedules, newsletters. ([boxoutsports.com/](https://boxoutsports.com/))
- **Mobile**: explicitly *not* a native app — it's a responsive website meant to be used via mobile browser (with a "add to home screen" shortcut suggested). This is confirmed by Box Out's own help docs, though rival Gipper's comparison page frames the lack of an app as a weakness (see §7). ([help.boxoutsports.com — mobile device article](https://help.boxoutsports.com/en-us/article/using-box-out-on-your-mobile-device-1jitpzz/))
- Company also touts a 1.2-second average render time per still graphic as a speed claim. ([boxoutsports.com/](https://boxoutsports.com/))

## 2. Business model and pricing

Straight subscription SaaS, three tiers, no free plan — only a 2-week free trial. Billed monthly or annually (annual = ~2 months free vs. monthly rate). All tiers include **unlimited users and unlimited teams**, which is a notable structural choice (no per-seat pricing) — relevant since a department has many coaches/SIDs/students touching the tool. ([boxoutsports.com/pricing](https://boxoutsports.com/pricing))

| Tier | Monthly | Annual | Templates | Video templates | Extras |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $60/mo | $480/yr ($40/mo equiv.) | 430+ | — | Unlimited uploads, unlimited users/teams |
| Plus | $175/mo | $1,500/yr | 1,930+ | 30+ (limited) | + Opponent/player database, limited background removal |
| Premium | $275/mo | $2,400/yr | 2,250+ | 130+ (all) | + Live stats integration, full background removal |

Switching incentive: if currently paying for a competing platform, Box Out will give the service free for the remainder of that competitor's active subscription term. ([boxoutsports.com/switch](https://boxoutsports.com/switch))

## 3. Target audience

Institutional, not individual-athlete. Every customer-facing page (features, testimonials, "for high schools," recruiting templates) targets **athletic departments and their staff** — athletic directors, coaches, sports information directors (SIDs), student managers — not athletes managing their own personal brand. Even the recruiting/"signing day" credential template is built for a *program* to announce a recruit, not for a player to self-promote. ([boxoutsports.com/athletic-directors](https://boxoutsports.com/athletic-directors), [boxoutsports.com/graphics/recruiting/credential](https://boxoutsports.com/graphics/recruiting/credential))

Segments named across the site and press: high schools, colleges (NAIA, NJCAA, and implicitly NCAA D1 given named customers), marketing agencies, minor league baseball, eSports orgs, and youth sports programs. ([boxoutsports.com/ai-info](https://boxoutsports.com/ai-info)) Notable named customers/logos: Notre Dame, Air Force Academy, Rutgers, Georgia Tech, Army West Point. ([boxoutsports.com/](https://boxoutsports.com/), [boxoutsports.com/graphics/recruiting/credential](https://boxoutsports.com/graphics/recruiting/credential)) These are mostly D1 programs — bigger and better-funded than the NAIA/D2/D3 segment central to Renan's hypothesis, though the NAIA/NJCAA governing-body deals (below) show real reach into the smaller-budget segment too.

**No evidence found that Box Out sells directly to individual athletes** for personal highlight/commitment graphics — that use case (per the project's CLAUDE.md hypothesis) appears to be unaddressed by this competitor, which is a potential opening.

## 5. Reviews and sentiment

This is the biggest data gap. **Box Out Sports has no discoverable presence on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot** — searches on all three (including `site:` searches) returned zero matching listings. Trustpilot's direct review URL for the domain returned a 403/blocked page (inconclusive, but no listing surfaced via search either). This is a real gap for prospective customers doing diligence, and likely reflects that its buyers (ADs, SIDs) don't use those review platforms as part of their purchase process. ([searches run against g2.com, capterra.com, trustpilot.com — no results])

- **Reddit**: no discussion threads found via multiple search queries (direct and `site:reddit.com`). Either genuinely low organic discussion volume, or discussions exist but aren't indexed/surfaced — treat as absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- **AlternativeTo.net**: has a listing but **zero user reviews or ratings**; page last updated 2019, so it's stale. It lists one comparable alternative, "SportFX Studio," a freemium desktop editor. ([alternativeto.net/software/boxoutsports](https://alternativeto.net/software/boxoutsports/))
- **Testimonials (company-published, so read as marketing, not independent review)**: Box Out's own site features quotes from ADs at Roger Bacon HS, Jacksonville State, and Buena Vista University, uniformly positive — praising ease of use, speed, and content-volume/follower growth ("tripled our content output"). ([boxoutsports.com/athletic-directors](https://boxoutsports.com/athletic-directors))
- **Competitor's counter-marketing** (Gipper's own "Box Out Sports vs Gipper" comparison page) is the single most useful "critical" source found, since real negative reviews don't otherwise exist publicly — see §7 for the specific claims, but treat them as one competitor's framing of another, not neutral fact. ([gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper](https://www.gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper))

**Bottom line on sentiment**: there is no independent, negative, or even neutral third-party review corpus for Box Out Sports anywhere online. All positive sentiment found is company-published; all critical sentiment found is rival-published. This itself is a notable finding.

## 7. Notable gaps, complaints, and unmet needs

Because no independent review corpus exists (§5), most of what follows comes from rival Gipper's direct comparison page, which should be read as adversarial marketing, not verified fact — but it's still informative about where a competitor sees an opening, and several claims are consistent with Box Out's own site (e.g., no native app is confirmed by Box Out's own help docs):

- **No native mobile app** — confirmed independently by Box Out's own help center (it's web-only/responsive). For a "post from the sideline right after the game" use case, a no-app browser workflow could be friction, especially for less tech-savvy coaches/volunteers with spotty stadium wifi. ([help.boxoutsports.com](https://help.boxoutsports.com/en-us/article/using-box-out-on-your-mobile-device-1jitpzz/), [gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper](https://www.gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper))
- **Editing requires re-rendering rather than live/instant visual feedback**, per Gipper's claim — if true, this cuts against the "speed + dead-simple usability" wedge, since a render-per-edit loop is a real friction point for fast-turnaround, in-the-moment posting. ([gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper](https://www.gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper))
- **Template lock-in / limited free-form editing** — Gipper claims Box Out restricts moving/resizing/adding/deleting elements more than Gipper does. Consistent with a "fill-in-the-blank template" product philosophy rather than a flexible editor — this is a plausible trade-off (simplicity vs. flexibility) rather than a clear defect.
- **Small team (2–10 employees per LinkedIn) supporting a large partnership footprint** (NAIA, NJCAA, 1,400+ programs via PrestoSports) — a plausible support/responsiveness bottleneck, though this is an inference, not a confirmed complaint pattern (no support-complaint reviews were actually found).
- **No public review-site presence (G2/Capterra/Trustpilot/Reddit)** is itself a gap: prospective buyers doing diligence have nowhere independent to check sentiment, which cuts both ways — it's also an opportunity for a new entrant to visibly "show its work" via testimonials/case studies where Box Out has none indexed.
- **No individual-athlete product** — Box Out is 100% institution-facing (teams/departments); nothing found addresses a player wanting their own commitment/highlight/senior-night graphic outside of a program doing it for them. This directly matches the gap the project's two-segment hypothesis (individual athletes + clubs) is trying to fill.
- **Pricing floor is real money for a small program**: even the cheapest tier is $480–$720/year, and the features programs most want (video templates, background removal, live stats) are gated to Plus/Premium at $1,500–$2,400/year — a genuinely small-budget NAIA/D2/D3 program may find even Basic a stretch, and the jump from Basic to Plus (~3x price) is steep for what's a meaningful feature gap (video, opponent database). This pricing cliff is a plausible wedge for a lower-cost or usage-based done-for-you offering.

## Key takeaways for our competitive positioning

1. **Box Out is a credible, entrenched, institution-only incumbent** — not a weak or dying competitor. Multi-year NAIA and NJCAA governing-body partnerships mean it already has default/preferred-vendor status with exactly the small-budget college segment this project is targeting. Any GTM into NAIA/NJCAA schools should assume some fraction are already Box Out customers or aware of the brand via those partnerships.
2. **The "speed + simplicity" edge needs sharpening, not abandoning.** Box Out already markets on speed/simplicity ("seconds not hours," "no design skill needed") — so simplicity alone isn't fully differentiated. The more defensible wedge, per the render-per-edit and template-lock-in claims (§7), may be "we do it *for* you" (a done-for-you service with a human/fast turnaround) vs. "here's yet another tool you still have to operate yourself," especially for the volunteer coach/small-athletic-office reality where nobody has time to learn even a simple tool.
3. **The individual-athlete segment looks genuinely open.** Box Out has zero visible presence serving individual players directly (personal commitment/highlight/senior-night graphics) — everything is department/program-facing. This validates that half of the project's two-segment hypothesis as unclaimed territory, at least by this competitor.
4. **Pricing gives room to position below Box Out on cost or above it on service**, since its per-year floor ($480–$2,400) is non-trivial for a genuinely small program, and Box Out has no free/cheap done-for-you or pay-per-graphic option — an on-demand, no-subscription "buy the graphics you need" model could appeal to the most budget-constrained programs and individual athletes/parents alike.
5. **The review/sentiment vacuum (no G2/Capterra/Trustpilot/Reddit presence) is a market-visibility opportunity.** A new entrant that actively collects and publishes real testimonials/case studies from day one would have more third-party credibility signal than the 10+ year incumbent currently has anywhere online.
6. **Verify, don't assume, on the "no mobile app" and "no live editing" gaps** — those specific claims came from a rival's marketing page, not neutral testing. Before leaning hard on "we have an app, they don't" as a pitch, it would be worth having someone actually trial Box Out's free 2-week trial to confirm these claims firsthand.

### Confidence summary
- **High confidence**: product features, pricing tiers, target audience/positioning, NAIA/NJCAA/PrestoSports partnerships (all sourced directly from company or governing-body press releases).
- **Medium confidence**: founding date (range, not exact year), employee count (LinkedIn self-reported, may be stale), funding status (thin data, inferred bootstrapped).
- **Low confidence / explicitly flagged estimates**: Instagram/Threads/Facebook follower counts (indexed snapshots, not live-verified), the excluded Glassdoor "Boxout" data (likely a different company), and all rival-sourced (Gipper) critical claims about product weaknesses.
