## 1. What the product/service does

Postgame is a **full-service NIL marketing agency**, not a self-serve app. It recruits and manages a network of 75,000+ college athlete influencers and runs paid influencer/content campaigns on behalf of brand clients (Adidas, Urban Outfitters, HEYDUDE, Gillette, Ruffles, Reebok, Crocs, CeraVe, Steve Madden, Tostitos, McDonald's, Taco Bell are cited as clients). Services include campaign strategy, athlete recruitment, negotiation, content production (from quick-turn UGC to premium videographer-produced content), NIL contract management, and performance reporting. ([home.pstgm.com](https://www.home.pstgm.com/), [home.pstgm.com/content](https://www.home.pstgm.com/content))

The companion mobile app (iOS/Android, "Postgame LLC") is athlete-facing: it acts as a rewards "wallet," manages affiliate/tracking links, ranks athletes by an internal "NIL Value" score, and helps athletes discover and accept brand campaign opportunities and manage compliance paperwork. It integrates with athletes' Instagram/TikTok/Twitter accounts to pull follower/engagement metrics. ([apps.apple.com/us/app/postgame-app/id1541500365](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/postgame-app/id1541500365), search-derived summary via [home.pstgm.com/appdownload](https://www.home.pstgm.com/appdownload))

Their content page claims "300,000+ unique pieces of content" produced by 30,000+ athletes for brand partners since the company's 2021 launch, spanning UGC to short/long-form elevated video. ([home.pstgm.com/content](https://www.home.pstgm.com/content))

There is **no evidence of a self-serve template/graphic-creation tool** for athletes or teams to design their own posts — content is produced through managed brand campaigns, not a DIY editor.

## 2. Business model and pricing

Postgame does not sell a subscription product to athletes or teams. Its revenue model is **agency commission/fees paid by brand clients** for running influencer campaigns (standard talent-agency economics) — no public pricing, rate card, or subscription tiers were found. ([home.pstgm.com](https://www.home.pstgm.com/) — page contains no pricing information; confirmed absent across all pages checked)

There is no free tier or per-post consumer pricing because athletes aren't the paying customer — brands are, and athletes are paid (not charged) to participate in campaigns.

## 3. Target audience

- **Primary customer (who pays): brands** seeking access to college-athlete influencers for marketing campaigns.
- **Primary user (supply side): individual college athletes** across 350+ colleges, not teams or athletic departments as institutional buyers. ([search-derived: "60,000 athlete users from more than 350 colleges"])
- No evidence of a team/club/athletic-department-as-customer offering — this is athlete-to-brand matchmaking, not an institutional social-media tool. This makes it a poor structural comparison to a "team/athletic department" design service targeting NAIA/D2/D3 programs.

## 7. Notable gaps / unmet needs a competitor could exploit

Important framing: because this Postgame is an NIL agency, not a graphic-design tool, its "gaps" are not directly transferable to a graphic-design-service competitor. That said, a few things are worth noting:

- **It only serves individual college athletes, not teams, clubs, or athletic departments.** There is no institutional/team product at all. Small-budget NAIA/D2/D3 athletic departments and their marketing/comms staff are entirely outside Postgame's addressable market — this is a structural white-space gap for a team-focused, done-for-you graphic service, but it's a gap versus this specific business, not proof of a gap in the graphic-design-tool market.
- **Scale/consistency of company-reported numbers is inconsistent** (25K vs. 60K vs. 75K athletes across different pages/timeframes), which suggests marketing-driven rather than audited figures — a reminder to discount vendor-reported scale claims generally when sizing any competitor.
- **Thin independent review footprint** (no G2/Capterra/Trustpilot/Reddit presence) means public sentiment data is not rich enough to mine for concrete user complaints about speed, usability, or turnaround — the three things most relevant to Renan's stated "speed + dead-simple usability" edge could not be validated or refuted against this company.
- **Diversification into NFTs/crypto ("NIL Coin")** signals a company stretching into speculative adjacent products rather than deepening its core service — could indicate management distraction, though this is inference, not confirmed fact.

## Key takeaways for our competitive positioning

1. **Verify the competitor before building a positioning strategy around it.** The "Postgame" that dominates search results is an NIL athlete-brand marketing agency (Sarasota, FL, founded 2020, ~2–24 employees depending on source, no public pricing, serves individual college athletes not teams) — a fundamentally different business model from a done-for-you graphic design service for teams/athletic departments. Building competitive strategy against this Postgame would be strategically misleading.
2. **The functionally-similar, actually-competing product appears to be Gipper** (gipper.com) — a self-serve subscription graphic-template platform explicitly targeting athletic departments/coaches, reportedly priced ~$19–49/mo per individual user based on third-party comparison pages, with thousands of templates. Other adjacent tools surfaced: Box Out Sports, Kickly, SportaPost, PostPlanify. **Recommend a follow-up research pass specifically on Gipper** if Renan wants a real apples-to-apples competitive teardown — that is likely the higher-value next step.
3. Because Postgame (the NIL agency) has no institutional/team offering, no self-serve editor, and no public pricing for a design product, none of its data (pricing, reviews, feature complaints) can be safely used as direct evidence for or against Renan's "speed + simplicity" wedge. Any positioning claims about "Postgame" should be dropped or re-scoped to Gipper/Box Out/Kickly until Renan confirms which product he actually meant.
4. If Renan did in fact mean this NIL-agency Postgame (e.g., because he's considering athlete-side NIL content creation as an adjacent opportunity, not just team/department graphics), that's a materially different market thesis — worth a quick clarifying conversation before more research spend goes into either direction.
