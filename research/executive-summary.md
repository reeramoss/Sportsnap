# Executive Summary — Sports Graphics Service Competitive Research

*Synthesized from: boxout-sports.md, postgame.md, gipper.md, market-scan.md (all in this folder). Research date: 2026-07-27.*

## TL;DR

The original "Postgame" competitor was a name collision (it's an NIL marketing agency, not a design tool) — the real analog is **Gipper**, which turns out to be a well-funded, entrenched player that already sponsors BOSCA, the actual conference for NAIA/D2/D3/NJCAA athletic administrators. Combined with Box Out Sports (also entrenched, NAIA+NJCAA official partner), the self-serve tool segment is more crowded and more embedded in the target buyer's world than assumed going in.

The "speed + dead-simple usability" edge, as originally framed, **does not hold up well against these specific competitors** — they already market and are reviewed as fast/easy, and are racing each other on exactly those terms. What the research does support, consistently across sources, is a sharper and different edge:

**Lead with delegation + reliable turnaround + price fit — "we do it for you, fast and reliably, without a $500+/year annual contract" — not "our tool is faster/simpler than theirs."**

## The competitive landscape (confirmed players)

| Competitor | Model | Price | Segment | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Box Out Sports** | Self-serve templates, web-only, no app | $60–275/mo | Institutions only | Official NAIA + NJCAA graphics partner; 10+ yrs old; no G2/Capterra/Trustpilot/Reddit presence at all |
| **Gipper** | Self-serve templates + video + native app | $625–3,000/**year** (no monthly) | Institutions only | ~$11–12M raised (Series A, Telescope Partners); sponsors BOSCA (the D2/D3/NAIA/NJCAA AD conference); App Store 2.9/5 (n=48), Play Store ~3.0/5 (n=13) |
| **Kickly** | Self-serve templates | $25/mo | Institutions | Positioned as "20x cheaper than a dedicated resource" |
| **SportaPost** | Mobile app | $9.95/mo (Pro) | Teams/clubs | Real paywall-backlash complaints when it moved from free to paid |
| **Canva + Etsy/Gumroad template packs** | DIY, one-time purchase | $10–30 one-time | DIY-willing buyers | Genuinely satisfies the "quick, decent graphic" need at very low cost |
| **Fiverr / Upwork freelancers** | Ad-hoc done-for-you (unbundled) | $5–10/gig or $27–48/hr | Anyone | Closest existing thing to "done-for-you" — but documented complaints: slow/unpredictable delivery, capped revisions, quality variance |
| **INFLCR / Opendorse** | NIL compliance + content distribution | N/A | Individual athletes | Not design tools — distribution/compliance layer only, not a real competitor |

**No true productized done-for-you service was found anywhere in this niche.** That gap is real and confirmed, not assumed.

## Why "speed + simplicity" needs to be dropped as the headline pitch

1. Every self-serve competitor (Gipper, Box Out, Kickly, Canva packs) already markets and is reviewed as easy — no independent complaints of complexity turned up anywhere. Gipper vs. Box Out is explicitly a fight over whose editor is faster/more real-time. A newcomer claiming "simpler" is entering a race the incumbents are already running against each other.
2. "Speed" has two different meanings, and only one is supported by evidence: speed of *editing a template* looks like a solved problem; speed/reliability of *turnaround when you hand it off to someone else* is genuinely unaddressed and is exactly where Fiverr/Upwork gig work shows real, documented pain.
3. No Reddit/X threads were found (in either direction) with someone explicitly complaining "this is too slow" or "too hard to use" about Gipper, Box Out, or Canva. Treat speed/simplicity as an *unvalidated* hypothesis, not a disproven one — but don't build the pitch on it without direct buyer conversations first.

## What the evidence actually supports

- **Staff bandwidth/delegation is the clearest, best-evidenced pain point.** The one piece of real practitioner commentary found (an AthleticDirectorU roundtable) shows departments responding to time pressure by cutting custom graphic work entirely — not by hunting for a better tool. Small "one-person shop" SIDs are the profile mentioned repeatedly. This is a delegation problem, not a tool-usability problem.
- **Price is a live, better-evidenced concern.** Gipper's annual-only $625–$3,000 contracts and SportaPost's paywall backlash both show buyers actively reacting to price/commitment structure.
- **The individual-athlete segment is confirmed open.** Neither Box Out nor Gipper has any evidence of serving individual players directly for personal commitment/highlight/senior-night graphics — both are 100% institution-facing. This validates that half of the original two-segment idea as real white space.
- **The review/sentiment vacuum is a visible-credibility opportunity.** None of Box Out, Gipper, Kickly, or SportaPost have a real G2/Capterra/Trustpilot presence. A new entrant that actively collects and publishes real testimonials/case studies from day one would out-credential all of them on public trust signal alone.

## Recommended positioning going forward

**"We produce your team's social graphics for you — fast, reliably, on a plan that doesn't lock you into $500+ upfront."**

- Compete against the *labor problem* (nobody on staff has time) and the *unreliable freelancer* workaround (Fiverr/Upwork), not against the self-serve tools' UX.
- Pricing: consider monthly or pay-per-batch pricing specifically because Gipper's annual-only billing is a real, named friction point for cash-strapped small programs — being the only option that lets someone "start this week, cancel anytime" is a genuine, evidenced wedge.
- Treat the individual-athlete segment (commitment graphics, senior night, highlight content) as a second, genuinely open lane — neither major competitor touches it, and it shares the same NAIA-network distribution as the player-passport idea.
- Before committing further: this is the point in the process to talk directly to a handful of real small-school SIDs/ADs (a handful of calls/DMs) to test the delegation+price framing — public search evidence couldn't confirm or refute it directly, and that's the single biggest open risk in this research.

## Confidence notes
Pricing, funding, and partnership facts (Box Out's NAIA/NJCAA deals, Gipper's Series A and BOSCA sponsorship) are high-confidence, sourced directly from company/press materials. Follower counts, revenue estimates, and customer-count claims are lower-confidence (self-reported or algorithmic estimates) and are flagged as such in the individual reports. No claim in this summary should be treated as more certain than the individual report it's drawn from.
