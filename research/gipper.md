# Competitor Research: Gipper (gipper.com)

Gipper is the real match for the "self-serve, template-based sports social graphics tool sold to athletic departments" that the earlier "Postgame" research pass was actually looking for — confirmed by multiple independent comparison articles that name Gipper as the category leader in this space. This report profiles Gipper directly.

---

## 1. What the product/service does

Gipper is a template-based content-creation platform, not a done-for-you design service — the user (coach, athletic director, communications staff) still does the assembly work, just with heavy pre-built scaffolding. It has three product lines ([gipper.com/ai-info-page](https://www.gipper.com/ai-info-page)):

- **Gipper Engage** — the core product. Users pick from a library of templates (reported sizes range from ~6,000 to 10,000+ depending on the page/date, plus a 17,000+ high-school/college logo database) for static graphics, motion/video graphics, and digital newsletters, covering game day, schedules, score updates, "athlete of the week," announcements, brackets, and stats. ([gipper.com](https://www.gipper.com/), [gipper.com/pricing](https://www.gipper.com/pricing))
- **Gipper Touch** — an interactive touchscreen kiosk product for hall-of-fame/recognition displays (hardware + software).
- **Gipper Display** — free digital signage for on-campus TVs.

**Workflow**: users save their school/team's official logo and colors once ("AutoBrand" one-click branding), then apply that branding automatically to any template. Editing is real-time/WYSIWYG (Gipper markets this as an advantage over at least one competitor, Box Out Sports, which requires re-rendering after each change). There's AI background removal, and users can connect unlimited Facebook/Instagram/X accounts to schedule and publish directly from the platform. There is also a native mobile app (iOS/Android) for creating and posting from a phone. ([gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper](https://www.gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper), [gipper.com/mobile-app](https://www.gipper.com/mobile-app))

Net: it's a Canva-like self-serve editor purpose-built for sports/school branding, not an agency that produces graphics on the customer's behalf.

## 2. Business model and pricing

Confirmed directly from Gipper's own pricing page and support docs ([gipper.com/pricing](https://www.gipper.com/pricing), [support.gogipper.com pricing article](https://support.gogipper.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410379990157-Gipper-pricing)):

- **Billing is annual (or multi-year) only — there is no monthly plan today.** This directly contradicts the "$19–49/mo" figure mentioned in the original brief. That figure does show up in some third-party comparison blogs (see below) but appears to be **outdated/stale marketing copy**, not current pricing.
- **Basic — $625/year**: up to 2 users, ~6,900+ templates, newsletters, logo database, scheduling, AI background removal, unlimited storage.
- **Pro — $1,500/year**: up to 30 users, ~8,900+ templates, collaboration tools, Pro-exclusive templates, removes Gipper branding from newsletters, premium support.
- **Premier — $3,000/year**: unlimited users, ~10,000+ templates, "Content Requests" feature, Premier-exclusive templates.
- **Gipper Touch**: custom/contact-sales pricing (hardware-inclusive, reportedly "a few thousand per year").
- **Gipper Display**: free ($0/year).
- **Free trial**: 14 days of the Premier tier, no credit card required.

Two third-party marketing blogs (both are competitors of Gipper — see Section 7) separately estimate all-in annual cost in the "$500–$2,000+" range and a 5-year cost of "$6,740," which roughly lines up with the official Basic/Pro tiers above. ([halloffamewall.com](https://halloffamewall.com/blog/gipper-vs-canva-free-alternative/), [touchhalloffame.us](https://touchhalloffame.us/blog/gipper-free-alternative-canva-for-schools/))

**No G2 or Capterra listing exists for Gipper** (confirmed by direct search — neither platform returns a Gipper product page), so no cross-checked, independent pricing/review data is available there.

## 3. Target audience

Gipper is institution-facing, not individual-athlete-facing. There is **no evidence Gipper sells to or markets toward individual athletes for personal branding/NIL purposes** — searches for "Gipper individual athlete NIL personal brand" returned nothing connecting Gipper to that use case. Its customer is always a team, school, district, or athletic department buying a seat-based/institutional license.

Confirmed customer segments, spanning youth through college ([gipper.com/customer-examples](https://www.gipper.com/customer-examples), [gipper.com/ai-info-page](https://www.gipper.com/ai-info-page)):

- **K-12 high schools** (the largest visible segment — e.g., St. Agnes School, Tampa Prep, Badin HS, Jenks HS)
- **School districts** (e.g., Owasso Public Schools, OKC Public Schools)
- **College athletics** — explicitly named customers include Columbia University, UC Santa Barbara, University of Hawai'i, Point Park University, Western Michigan, Seton Hall — and Gipper has a formal sponsorship of BOSCA, described as the only professional-development event specifically for athletic administrators at **D2, D3, NAIA, and NJCAA** programs. This is a strong, direct signal that Gipper actively courts the small-budget college segment Renan is targeting. ([gipper.com/blog/gipper-signs-partnership...bosca](https://www.gipper.com/blog/gipper-signs-partnership-to-become-become-official-sponsor-of-bosca))
- **Club/youth sports** (Stars Basketball Club, Tulsa Soccer Club, LA Breakers FC, OVR USA Volleyball)

So Gipper is squarely present in the D2/D3/NAIA space Renan cares about — it is not a gap left open by them.

## 7. Notable gaps, complaints, and unmet needs (competitive opportunity)

This is the most important section for Renan's "speed + dead-simple usability as a service" hypothesis. Two sources of complaints surfaced, both of which are competitor marketing content and should be read with that bias flagged, but the *specific, falsifiable* claims within them are still useful signal:

**From Box Out Sports (a direct paid competitor) and its "Gipper alternative" landing page** ([boxoutsports.com/gipper-alternative](https://boxoutsports.com/gipper-alternative)):
- Users reportedly switched from Gipper to Box Out because Box Out lets you generate **one graphic in multiple sizes/aspect ratios simultaneously**, which they say "saves hours" — implying Gipper does not do this well and still costs meaningful per-post time even with templates.
- Claims Gipper's mobile experience "requires app" / isn't as mobile-friendly, and that its output/design quality is "just like Canva" rather than more premium, professionally-designed work.
- Claims Gipper is "less responsive" on customer service and costs more per month equivalent ($52 vs Box Out's $40) — though note Gipper doesn't actually sell monthly, so this comparison may be normalizing annual price to a monthly figure.
- Gipper's own rebuttal page ([gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper](https://www.gipper.com/boxout-sports-vs-gipper)) concedes the multi-size-export point isn't addressed and instead argues real-time WYSIWYG editing and a broader template library are more important — it does **not** claim to solve the "make my whole week of content for me" done-for-you use case at all.

**From HallOfFameWall / TouchHOF (Rocket Alumni Solutions' competing hall-of-fame/display marketing blogs)** ([halloffamewall.com](https://halloffamewall.com/blog/gipper-vs-canva-free-alternative/), [touchhalloffame.us](https://touchhalloffame.us/blog/gipper-free-alternative-canva-for-schools/)):
- Positioned mainly as "just use free Canva instead" sales pitches, so treat skeptically, but the specific complaints — narrow focus on sports graphics only, limited video sophistication, "vendor lock-in" needing multiple tools for a full comms need (academic recognition, admin communications) — are consistent with Gipper being a narrow, template-only tool rather than a full content/marketing solution.

**Cross-cutting theme relevant to Renan's thesis**: every source, including Gipper's own marketing, frames Gipper's value as "a faster **self-serve editor**," not as anyone actually producing the graphics *for* the customer. The recurring complaint pattern across App Store reviews and the Box Out comparison is that even with templates, the athletic director/coach still has to be the one sitting down, picking the template, resizing it for each platform, and posting it — Gipper has not eliminated that labor, only reduced it. Nobody in this research surfaced a "fully done-for-you" competitor in this exact niche. That is consistent with a real, unaddressed gap: a service where a human/ops team turns around finished, on-brand graphics for a small-budget program with near-zero effort from the AD/coach, rather than handing them a (still non-trivial) template editor. Given Gipper's small App/Play Store rating base (48 and 13 ratings respectively) and modest Instagram following (~3.4K) relative to its "20,000+ customers" claim, this also suggests Gipper's user base, while numerically large by seat count, is not deeply vocal/evangelical online — there's room for a challenger with a genuinely differentiated model to make noise.

## Key takeaways for our competitive positioning

1. **Gipper is the real, well-funded, entrenched competitor** in exactly the D2/D3/NAIA/small-athletic-department space Renan is targeting — it already sponsors the main conference for that buyer segment (BOSCA) and has named customers at that level. This is not a gap Renan would be first into.
2. **Gipper's pricing floor ($625/year, ~2 users) is a real, low-budget-friendly price point** — a "faster/simpler" service will need to either beat this price at low commitment, or justify a premium via a fundamentally different (done-for-you, no-effort) value proposition rather than competing on template count or price alone.
3. **The core exploitable gap is labor, not price or template selection**: Gipper is still a self-serve editor — someone on staff must still choose templates, customize, resize per platform, and schedule. No evidence surfaced of Gipper (or its competitors Box Out, Aktivate, Greenfly) offering an actual done-for-you production service. This is the clearest, most defensible point of differentiation for Renan's "speed + simplicity as a service" hypothesis — the pitch isn't "our editor is easier than Gipper's," it's "you don't touch an editor at all."
4. **Gipper's public review signal is thin and mixed, not a moat.** 2.9/5 (App Store, n=48) and ~3.0/5 (Play Store, n=13) are unremarkable for a company claiming 20,000+ customers, and there's no G2/Capterra/Trustpilot presence to build trust against. A new entrant with strong word-of-mouth and review-collection discipline could out-credential Gipper relatively quickly in public review signal, even without matching its scale.
5. **Annual-only billing is a friction point Gipper has chosen to keep** (no monthly option) — a "start this week, cancel anytime" or monthly/pay-per-batch pricing model could be a meaningful wedge for cash-strapped small programs that don't want to commit $625–$3,000 upfront sight-unseen.
6. **Caveat on all scale/financial figures**: Gipper's own customer/user counts are internally inconsistent across its marketing pages (4,000+ institutions vs. 20,000+ "users"/"customers"/"athletic programs" used seemingly interchangeably), and the only revenue/employee figures available (Growjo: ~$4.4M revenue, 38 employees) are explicitly algorithmic estimates, not disclosed financials. Treat Gipper's true scale as "a real, venture-backed (~$11-12M raised), multi-year-old company with meaningful institutional penetration," but don't anchor hard to any single headcount or revenue number found here.
