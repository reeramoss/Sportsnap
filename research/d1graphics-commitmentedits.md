# Competitor Research: D1 Graphics (d1graphics.co) & Commitment Edits (commitmentedits.com)

Researched via public/logged-out web browsing only (no accounts created, no logins). All Instagram data below reflects the logged-out public profile view, which does not expose per-post like/comment counts — those figures could not be obtained without logging in.

---

## 1. Relationship between D1 Graphics and Commitment Edits

**Conclusion: these appear to be two genuinely independent, competing companies — not a rebrand, sub-brand, or whitelabel relationship — despite overlapping product naming that creates the appearance of a connection.**

Evidence:

- **d1graphics.co/commitment-edits is not a redirect to commitmentedits.com.** It is a native page hosted on D1Graphics' own domain. "Commitment Edits" is simply D1Graphics' own marketing name for its core commitment-graphic product line (their nav bar lists "Commitment Edits," "Recruiting Graphics," and "Top Offer Graphics" as three in-house product pages). The combined page title "Commitment Graphics | Commitment Edits – Any Sport, Any Level" is D1Graphics' own SEO title tag for that page, not evidence of shared ownership with the separate commitmentedits.com site. ([d1graphics.co/commitment-edits](https://www.d1graphics.co/commitment-edits))
- **Different legal entities.** D1graphics.co's footer reads "© 2026 D1Graphics LLC" ([d1graphics.co](https://www.d1graphics.co/)). Commitmentedits.com's Terms of Service reads "© 2025 NG Design and Media LLC. All rights reserved," and commitmentedits.com is built on Shopify under that entity ([commitmentedits.com/policies/terms-of-service](https://commitmentedits.com/policies/terms-of-service)).
- **Different founders.** D1Graphics' co-founder is Elias Feldman, based in New York, per his LinkedIn profile ([linkedin.com/in/elias-feldman](https://www.linkedin.com/in/elias-feldman/)). Commitmentedits.com/NG Design and Media LLC is owned by Nick Giesinger, whose LinkedIn describes him as "Enhancing the Online Identity of College Athletics Programs across the Country" ([linkedin.com/in/nick-giesinger-68b8a3228](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nick-giesinger-68b8a3228/)).
- **"Commitment edits" is a generic industry term**, not a proprietary brand exclusive to either site — an unrelated Etsy seller lists "COMMITMENT EDITS" graphics ([etsy.com/listing/1605004491](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1605004491/commitment-edits-college-commitment)), and sports photographer Jeff Palicki runs his own, separate "Commitment Edits" service page ([jeffpalickiphotography.com/commitment-edits](https://www.jeffpalickiphotography.com/commitment-edits)). This confirms the phrase is category-standard language, which explains the naming collision without implying affiliation.
- **Separate Instagram brand identities** with different follower counts, different bios, and different visual styles (detailed in section 8) — consistent with two separate operators rather than one company running two front-ends.

**Caveat:** I could not run a formal WHOIS lookup or find SEC/state business-registration filings confirming beneficial ownership, so an undisclosed backend/reseller relationship cannot be 100% ruled out. But every piece of public evidence found (distinct LLCs, distinct named founders, distinct copyright years, distinct Shopify vs. custom-site tech stacks, distinct Instagram accounts) points to independent competitors, not one company. Treat this as a well-supported conclusion, not a certainty.

## 2. What the product/service actually is

Both companies sell **individual athlete recruiting/commitment-milestone graphics**, not broader team/program content, and both are **static-image-first with a shallow "video" add-on** rather than a true motion-graphics product.

**D1Graphics** — three product lines ([d1graphics.co](https://www.d1graphics.co/), [d1graphics.co/commitment-edits](https://www.d1graphics.co/commitment-edits), [d1graphics.co/top-offer-graphics](https://www.d1graphics.co/top-offer-graphics), [d1graphics.co/recruiting-graphics](https://www.d1graphics.co/recruiting-graphics)):
- **Commitment Graphics** — the flagship product, announcing a college commitment.
- **Top Offer Graphics** — graphics highlighting scholarship/recruiting offers received.
- **Recruiting Graphics** — stat/camp/metric highlight graphics an athlete uses to promote themselves to coaches.

**Commitment Edits** — three collections plus add-ons ([commitmentedits.com](https://commitmentedits.com/)):
- **Commitment Designs** — the flagship product.
- **Offer Designs** — offer-announcement graphics.
- **Signing Designs** — National Signing Day graphics.
- **Jersey Swap** — a distinct add-on/editing service at both companies, digitally placing the athlete in the new school's uniform when no real photo exists.

**On video/motion:** Neither company sells standalone motion graphics. At D1Graphics, "Video" is a paid add-on ($20) that pairs a short, templated intro-video style (19 named options, e.g. "Video #1"–"#19") with the static graphic, set to a song of the buyer's choice ([d1graphics.co/commitment-edits](https://www.d1graphics.co/commitment-edits)). At Commitment Edits, the same pattern applies: 8 named video-intro themes (Logo Outline, Digital Glitch, Phone Intro, Earth Zoom, Bank Vault, Futuristic, Dungeon, Call) can be added to any style, and the FAQ describes designs as "presented as photo only but the video version can be added on when customizing" ([commitmentedits.com/collections/commitment-edits](https://commitmentedits.com/collections/commitment-edits)). This is a templated intro clip bolted onto a static image, not custom-cut motion content.

Neither company offers game-day, senior-night, schedule-release, roster, or sponsor graphics — the catalog is scoped entirely to individual recruiting/commitment milestones.

## 4. Turnaround time / delivery process — manual production CONFIRMED

**Renan's hypothesis is confirmed directly from both companies' own FAQ/process language: these are human-executed, made-to-order services, not automated self-serve tools, and the ~24-hour figure he observed is the paid expedite tier, not the default.**

**D1Graphics**, from its own FAQ ([d1graphics.co/commitment-edits](https://www.d1graphics.co/commitment-edits)):
- Standard turnaround is stated as **"typically 2–3 days," varying by season**, with jersey swaps/add-ons taking "a little bit longer."
- Express delivery is a **paid add-on ($10–$20 depending on product) that guarantees 24 hours** — meaning 24 hours is the accelerated tier, not the norm.
- Explicitly: **no preview before purchase** — "we ensure that each of our graphics are as high quality as possible and spend a substantial amount of time on each one."
- Jersey swaps are called out as taking "a significant amount of time to complete" per swap.
- Delivery is via **email with a download link** after the graphic is finished — a one-way, order-then-wait flow, not a live editor.

**Commitment Edits**, from its own FAQ ([commitmentedits.com/pages/faq](https://commitmentedits.com/pages/faq)):
- Standard turnaround is **2–3 days**; next-day costs $15 extra; same-day (cutoff 7pm EST) costs $25 extra.
- Delivery is **"sent over email to the corresponding email entered at checkout."**
- Revisions are open-ended and manual: **"If you would like any changes or adjustments, we will make them until you are completely satisfied"** — language consistent with a human designer iterating on a file, not a self-serve tool regenerating instantly.
- No refunds once a paid request is submitted, except at the company's discretion if cancelled before work begins — again implying a human starts working on each order individually.

**Net assessment:** both are order-form-in, human-designed, email-out workflows with no live preview or self-serve editor. This is a real scalability ceiling — quality and turnaround are both bottlenecked by designer hours per order, and both companies monetize speed as a premium add-on rather than solving it structurally.

## 5. Catalog breadth and customization

- **D1Graphics:** 17 static graphic styles for its Commitment Graphic product (Style #1–#17, with #16–#17 marked "NEW"), 19 named video-intro add-on options, and jersey-swap capability described as working for "almost every school, regardless of sport or division." Its public showcase/search tool lists 216 sample commitment graphics spanning at least 16 sports (basketball, baseball, football, soccer, volleyball, softball, XC/TF, hockey, lacrosse, wrestling, golf, rowing, swim/dive, cheer, tennis, pickleball) and 12 filterable graphic styles in that tool specifically ([d1graphics.co](https://www.d1graphics.co/), [d1graphics.co/commitment-edits](https://www.d1graphics.co/commitment-edits)).
- **Commitment Edits:** 25 styles in the core Commitment Designs collection (all $30 flat), 5 styles in Signing Designs, plus a separate Offer Designs collection, and 8 named video-intro themes ([commitmentedits.com/collections/commitment-edits](https://commitmentedits.com/collections/commitment-edits), [commitmentedits.com/collections/signing-day-edits](https://commitmentedits.com/collections/signing-day-edits)). One page states "20+ available graphics" total, which appears to undercount the 25 shown in the Commitment collection alone — possibly stale marketing copy.
- **Customization at both** is limited to filling in fixed template fields — athlete name, school, position/sport, signature, personal statement text, and (as an add-on) jersey swap — rather than open design freedom, color picker, or logo/branding tools. Neither offers automatic team-color/logo branding of the kind Gipper markets ("AutoBrand").

## 6. Target audience

Both companies are **individual athlete/family-facing**, not team/program/coach-facing. D1Graphics' "Recruiting Graphics" line is stats/camp highlight content, but it is still built for and marketed to an individual athlete trying to get recruited — not a coach or athletic department managing a program's social presence. Neither site offers multi-seat/team accounts, bulk-roster tools, or athletic-department branding kits. This is the clearest structural difference from Gipper, Box Out Sports, and Postgame (per the existing competitive research in this folder), which are institution-facing tools sold to athletic departments and coaches. D1Graphics and Commitment Edits instead compete directly in the individual-athlete "commitment/offer/signing announcement" niche that SportSnap is also targeting.

## 9. Reviews / sentiment

**No solid independent third-party review data was found for either company.** Searches for Trustpilot, Google Business reviews, BBB profiles, Reddit threads, and recruiting-parent forum discussions specifically naming "D1Graphics"/"d1graphics.co" or "Commitment Edits"/"commitmentedits.com" did not surface any independently hosted reviews or complaint threads. Commitment Edits' own site claims a "5-star Google rating," but I could not locate a Google Business Profile to verify this independently — treat it as an unverified self-reported claim. All testimonials referenced in section 2/3 are self-hosted on each company's own website and should be read as curated marketing copy, not independent sentiment. I did not find evidence of scam complaints either, but general web searches did surface unrelated broader warnings about recruiting-services scams (a different category of service — paid "recruiting exposure" services, not done-for-you graphics) that are worth being aware of as adjacent noise in this search space but do not implicate either company specifically.

**This is a genuine information gap** — the absence of findable independent reviews should not be read as evidence of either good or bad reputation, only as an absence of easily discoverable public review data for either business at the time of this research.

## 10. Notable gaps/weaknesses a faster, more automated, broader competitor could exploit

- **Manual, non-scalable production model at both companies** (confirmed in section 4): both explicitly state they "spend a substantial amount of time on each order," offer no live preview, and deliver via one-off email after a human finishes the file. Standard turnaround is 2–3 days at both; 24-hour/same-day is a paid upsell, not the default. A genuinely faster default turnaround — or a real self-serve/AI-assisted instant-preview tool — is a clear, provable differentiator.
- **Shallow "video" offering.** At both companies, "video" means a short templated intro clip bolted onto a static image, not real custom motion/animated content. A true motion-graphics product would be new ground neither currently covers.
- **Single-purpose catalog scoped only to recruiting milestones** (commitment/offer/signing day). Neither serves the ongoing content needs of an athlete's broader journey (e.g., regular stat updates, off-season highlight content, general personal-brand/NIL content) or a program/team's ongoing needs (game day, senior night, schedule releases) — both gaps SportSnap could address with a broader catalog.
- **Template-locked customization** — no automatic team-color/logo-aware branding system; buyers just fill in text fields on a fixed style. A more flexible, brand-aware customization layer (à la Gipper's AutoBrand, applied to the individual-athlete use case) is an open opportunity.
- **Inconsistent self-reported numbers and unverifiable institutional claims** at both companies (Commitment Edits' 8,000 vs. 10,000+ discrepancy; D1Graphics' unverified "used by Ohio State, Baylor, Texas" claims) with **no findable independent reviews** (Trustpilot/Google/BBB) at either — an opening for a competitor that builds a verifiable, transparent review presence and clear, consistent numbers to win trust with recruiting-anxious parents doing due diligence.
- **Small, likely bootstrapped operations** (no funding, employee counts, or press found for either) — meaningfully smaller and less capitalized than Gipper, suggesting this niche (individual athlete commitment graphics) is being served by scrappy, manual-labor shops rather than a well-funded, technically sophisticated player — a gap a better-resourced, automation-first entrant could win on speed and consistency alone.

## Key takeaways for SportSnap's positioning

1. **Speed is the single most defensible wedge.** Both competitors' own FAQs confirm 2–3 day standard turnaround with paid expedites to reach 24 hours or same-day — because both are manual, one-designer-at-a-time operations. If SportSnap can deliver comparable or better quality via a faster (ideally automated or heavily template-assisted) pipeline as the *default*, not an upsell, that is a provable, FAQ-quotable advantage over both.
2. **A real motion/video product is white space.** Neither company sells actual custom motion graphics — both only offer a templated intro-clip add-on layered onto a static image. If SportSnap's plan includes genuine static + motion capability, that's a categorical upgrade neither competitor currently matches, not just an incremental one.
3. **Catalog breadth beyond recruiting milestones is open ground.** Both competitors are narrowly scoped to commitment/offer/signing-day graphics for individual athletes. SportSnap could differentiate either by going deeper on that same individual-athlete recruiting journey (more moments covered — camp updates, season stat drops, NIL content) or broader into team/program content (competing more directly with Gipper/Box Out/Postgame) — either direction is unaddressed by D1Graphics/Commitment Edits today.
4. **Pricing should be transparent and competitively anchored, not necessarily lower.** D1Graphics' base commitment graphic is $40 (+add-ons); Commitment Edits' is a flat $30. Given both are already priced modestly and both monetize speed via upsells, SportSnap doesn't need a race-to-the-bottom price — it needs to make default (not upsell) turnaround and broader catalog the value story, potentially at a comparable or even slightly premium price if quality/speed is visibly better.
5. **Trust-building is undefended territory.** Neither competitor has a findable independent review presence (no Trustpilot/Google Business reviews located), and Commitment Edits has visibly inconsistent self-reported customer-count claims. A competitor that actively builds verifiable social proof (real, checkable reviews; consistent numbers; genuine institutional partnerships if pursued) can win the trust battle with recruiting-anxious parents doing exactly the kind of due-diligence research this report itself represents.
